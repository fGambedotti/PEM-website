Chart 1: mk_story_1_fuel_poverty_map.png
This map shows the baseline fuel poverty rate by LSOA across Milton Keynes, before adding energy communities.
Darker polygons indicate higher fuel poverty rates, and lighter polygons indicate lower rates.
The latitude/longitude grid is included so viewers can place each area geographically.

Chart 2: mk_story_2_deployment_map_sd.png
This map shows where energy communities are deployed in the SD scenario, with bubble size and color representing MW per LSOA.
Light gray points/polygons are in-scope LSOAs without active deployment in this scenario, while green bubbles mark deployed locations.
In this run, 30 out of 166 Milton Keynes LSOAs are active, with 371.84 MW total modelled capacity.

Chart 3: mk_story_3_outcomes_sd.png
This chart shows three model outcome proxies over time (2025 to 2050): fuel-poverty reduction proxy, flexibility (peak MW reduced), and demand-reduction proxy.
The 100% value in fuel-poverty reduction is a model-normalized proxy ceiling in this rollout table, not a literal statement that real fuel poverty is completely eliminated.
The demand-reduction proxy reaches about 2.42%, which means modelled demand is reduced by ~2.42% versus its baseline proxy under this scenario.

Chart 4: mk_story_4_gini_targeting.png
The green bars are deployment MW Gini values by scenario, where higher values indicate a more spatially concentrated deployment pattern.
The dashed line is the baseline Gini of fuel-poverty exposure (proxy), shown as a context line for inequality in need rather than inequality in deployment.
The orange targeting-ratio line compares MW share in the highest-poverty 30% LSOAs to their population share; values above 1 indicate progressive targeting toward higher-need areas.
