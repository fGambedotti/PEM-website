What is this project about?

Energy communities, locally governed groups that collectively invest in low-carbon energy, are central to a just energy transition. But we still lack good answers to a fundamental question: where should they be built, and for whom?

This PhD project develops an optimisation model that determines how energy communities should be deployed across England and Wales, accounting not just for cost, but for equity, fuel poverty, grid constraints, and community governance. Crucially, the assumptions that drive the model are co-developed with practitioners through structured workshops, ensuring that real-world knowledge shapes the analysis from the start.

Research Questions

1

Where and at what scale should energy communities be deployed across England and Wales to maximise social, environmental, and economic benefits?

2

How do different policy priorities, such as emphasising cost efficiency versus equity targeting, change who benefits from energy community deployment?

3

How can qualitative knowledge from practitioners be systematically translated into quantitative modelling assumptions, and what difference does it make?

4

What is the measurable impact of community-based energy deployment compared to individual household technology adoption alone?

How the research works

The project follows a participatory modelling approach: practitioner knowledge directly shapes the model before results are generated, not after.

1

Baseline model

An initial optimisation model is built using publicly available data on energy, housing, and deprivation.

Status: in progress (April 2026)

2

Pre-workshop survey

A short survey gathers practitioners' initial views on key assumptions and priorities.

Date: 6 May 2026

3

Participatory workshop

A structured two-hour discussion explores key themes to refine the model's assumptions.

Date: 3 June 2026, 2:00-4:00 PM (UK time)

4

Scenario modelling

Insights are translated into model parameters, and scenarios reflecting different priorities are run.

Window: June-September 2026

5

Second participatory workshop

Reflection and feedback session to validate findings and challenge assumptions before final analysis.

Window: September-October 2026
